Case Study

Measuring AI Adoption

A three-agent AI system that helps bank associates use AI on real projects safely.

Tools: IBM watsonx Orchestrate, IBM Bob, IBM Agent Development Kit, Python, GitHub

Overview

Measuring AI Adoption is a three-agent AI system that helps bank associates use AI on real projects safely. It plans the work, blocks sensitive data automatically, and drafts research summaries where every number has a source. I designed, built, tested and documented the whole system myself.

My Role

I owned the project from start to finish. I framed the problem, held conversations with professionals in the financial services workforce, designed the solution, built the agents in watsonx Orchestrate, used IBM Bob to write the code, tested the system with real public data, and produced the demo video and final submission.

The Problem

Banks are investing heavily in AI, but many employees don't know where it fits in their daily work or what data is safe to share with it. So they lose time, do the work by hand, or turn to unapproved tools that put company and customer data at risk.

The research backs this up. 45% of workers used unapproved AI tools in the past month, and 34% don't know which tools their employer approves (WalkMe, 2026). And 77% of employees say AI tools have added to their workload (The Financial Brand).

In my conversations with professionals in the financial services workforce, one theme kept coming up: the hard part isn't getting AI tools, it's knowing how to apply them to real work safely.

Who It's For

Bank associates at any level who work on projects with stakeholder deliverables, like research summaries and strategy decks. They're expected to use AI, but they often haven't had role-specific training, and they worry about making mistakes with sensitive data. Early-career associates often know the most about AI tools, but they have the least experience and guidance on applying them to real work safely.

The Solution

A three-agent system in IBM watsonx Orchestrate that keeps the associate in charge while AI handles the routine work.

  1. Planner: reads the associate's project brief, splits the work into what AI will do and what the associate will do, and waits for approval.
  2. Data Guardrail Agent: checks every data source. Approved public data goes through; confidential or personal data is blocked with an explanation.
  3. Research and Drafting Agent: uses a custom Python tool to count the data exactly and writes a summary with a source for every number.

The associate then adds their own recommendations and gives final approval.

The associate starts and finishes every project; confidential data never reaches the Drafting Agent.

How I Built It

I built all three agents in watsonx Orchestrate on the GPT-OSS 120B model and deployed the system live as version 2.0.0. The Planner hands work to the other two agents as collaborators.

IBM Bob was my main development tool. It set up the project folders and Git, wrote a script that cleaned the raw CFPB download into a balanced sample, wrote the count_complaints tool, and created a fake confidential file to test the guardrail. I approved each of Bob's actions one at a time and checked its results before moving on.

Testing and Results

I tested the system on 498 public CFPB credit card complaints from the last six months, 166 each for JPMorgan Chase, Citibank and Bank of America.

What I Learned

I began this project with experience using AI tools, but none building with them. Through IBM's AI Experiential Learning Lab, I learned to design agents, develop Python tools, and manage code through the command line and GitHub, building each skill as the project required it.

My key insight is that the way I learned mirrors the way this system teaches: rather than mastering everything up front, associates learn where AI fits through each plan they approve, making learning part of their daily work instead of a separate training.

What's Next

  • Bank systems: connect to a bank's own data permissions and compliance policies.
  • More deliverables: support decks, financial models and reports.
  • Coaching mode: explain why each task went to AI, so associates learn the reasoning.
  • Measurable impact: track time saved, unapproved tool use avoided, and growth in AI confidence.
